# Arabic, English, Russian and multilingual editorial QA

A translation is a new **complete editorial edition**, not a word substitution exercise. Every delivered language receives its own language-aware content review, Word direction test, updated visual captions, and final page-by-page render check.

## All languages: nonnegotiable
1. Preserve original factual claims, qualifiers, uncertainty, quote scope and every source ID. Do not silently change dates, units, examples, evidence strength or numerical precision between editions.
2. Localize syntax, punctuation, headings, glossary, tables, chart labels, callouts, footer text and the table of contents, not just body paragraphs. Retain source titles in their original published language unless a legitimate published translation exists or a translated title is clearly marked as supplied for readability.
3. Use established domain terminology. At first mention give localized term with the original technical term where useful; maintain a terminology map across headings, visuals and snippets. Keep literal code, file paths, identifiers, URLs and commands unchanged unless the lesson explicitly teaches translated placeholders.
4. Check locale-specific typography, numeral convention, decimal separators when appropriate and unit consistency. Never introduce a different data result by converting/rounding badly. If reviewer expertise isn't sufficient, disclose need for specialist native/professional review rather than claiming perfection.

## Arabic
- Set section/paragraph RTL and complex-script language properly, not only right alignment. Mirror TOC indentation, note stripe, page decoration, flowchart reading sequence and header/footer where appropriate.
- Preserve English technical terms and abbreviations **in original English spelling** inside Arabic text when conventional or requested, for example `Table of Contents`, `HTML`, `Python`, `API`; introduce an Arabic explanation rather than translating code identifiers.
- Treat embedded Latin phrases, references, DOI/URL, mathematical notation and code as explicit LTR spans, and verify punctuation and bracket placement around them after pagination and rendering. Avoid broken ligatures, justification stretching, contradictory digit direction and misordered citation markers.
- Use natural Modern Standard Arabic for articles unless the user requests a different register. Audit hamza, alif, taa marbuta, plural agreement, logical connectives, punctuation and explanatory clarity; do not overuse diacritics except where they clarify teaching content.

## English
- Select US or UK English by brief and maintain one spelling system except in source names/quotations. Verify articles, agreement, concise academic tone, natural paragraph transitions and consistent capitalization in headings, TOC and captions.
- Technical phrases retain their established industry spelling; translate Arabic/Russian examples only when meaning and cultural frame survive. Code/URLs stay literal.

## Russian
- Maintain grammatical case, aspect, verb agreement, gender, number and idiomatic academic/professional tone; check accepted field terminology rather than literal English calques. Confirm use of `ё` per project convention and consistently apply Russian typography, quotation marks (`«...»` when appropriate), punctuation and spacing.
- Verify all Cyrillic characters and mixed Latin/Cyrillic strings in chosen fonts. Keep Latin programming terms or international abbreviations intact when required. Do not claim native-speaker verification unless actually performed.

## Parallel or multiple editions
- Create an edition matrix with language, direction, spelling standard, termbase, figures/callout translations, bibliography convention, required text length changes and review status. Word pagination/TOC is generated **afresh per edition**; do not reuse the Arabic TOC page numbers for English or Russian.
- For a **single mixed-language document**, style each language run and appropriately isolate LTR/RTL blocks. For separate DOCX editions, perform independent page rendering and at least spot-check key matched paragraphs, all tables, source IDs and figure legends across editions.
