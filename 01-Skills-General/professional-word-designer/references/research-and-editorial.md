# Research integrity, human-quality editing, and citations

## Source ledger before composition
Record for each usable source: numeric source ID (assigned on first citation), author/institution, exact title, publisher/site or journal, date/year, edition/pages when available, persistent DOI or verified URL, publication context, content claims it supports, and any uncertainty or known limitations. Use primary sources when feasible, especially for regulations, scientific experiments, statistics, and product standards. Distinguish a source's opinion/interpretation from verified findings. Never invent an author, quotation, study, DOI, page number or access date. Cite dated material appropriately rather than presenting it as newly verified current information.

## Natural and rigorous educational voice
- Open with the actual learning question, not formulaic statements about an “ever-evolving world.” Organize by the reader's conceptual needs; move logically from context to explanations to examples or contrast and implications.
- Vary sentence shape naturally and locally. Every paragraph should have a job: describe, explain cause, show evidence, compare, qualify, demonstrate, or synthesize. Remove empty transitions, exaggerated adjectives, redundant “key takeaways”, and robotic repetitions.
- Use concrete, appropriate examples without inventing lived experience, field interviews, first-person experiments or supposed eyewitness accounts. Label hypothetical examples as examples. Human editor quality is a process, not a promise about AI-detector results.
- Technical terminology should be precise and consistently defined, including target language spelling and English original in parentheses when appropriate. Honor requested audience level without sacrificing accuracy.
- Split unusually dense paragraphs, but avoid fragmented single-sentence filler. Headings should reflect meaningful conceptual sections, not mechanically divide every 100 words.
- Keep author identity transparent if requested; do not fabricate a human byline, credentials, or institutional review. If disclosure of AI assistance is required by publisher/school, follow it.

## Paragraph-level in-text source convention
Default when no required style is supplied: **numbered bracket citations in first-appearance order**, e.g., `[1]`, `[1, 3]`, `[2–4]`; reuse an ID for repeated uses of the same source. Put citation immediately after the supported statement or near the end of the relevant sourced paragraph, according to target style. A paragraph with multiple materially distinct claims may require multiple citations attached to respective claims, not an indiscriminate single reference at the end. An introduction, editorial transition, explanation of the author's document plan, or mathematical derivation not taken from elsewhere may not need a citation. Every citation must correspond to the numbered bibliography; every bibliography entry should be cited in the body unless a separate “Further reading” section is intentionally used.

Where native Word can reliably link markers, hyperlink a bracket marker to a bookmark at the bibliography entry; do not compromise layout to force hyperlinks. Valid footnotes/endnotes can supplement the numeric style for clarification if requested; never silently mix competing citation systems. For direct quotations, preserve exact wording, attribution and a locator/page where available; use quotations sparingly and respect usage rights. For re-created figures/tables, mark “Prepared from [n]”; for copied licensed figures, provide required attribution/license details.

## Bibliography record example (format demonstration only)
`[1] Author or institution. Title. Publisher/Journal, year. DOI or URL (verified).`
This is a **schema**, not a fabricated real source. Fully populate only verified fields; do not fill unknown details by guesswork.

## Final review passes
1. **Substantive edit:** verify outline logic, definitions, completeness, contrast between fact and inference, limitations and audience level.
2. **Citation audit:** trace each consequential factual claim to evidence, each chart datum to a source, each bracket number to an entry and each external image to rights provenance; flag any unverified part.
3. **Copyedit:** grammar, reference consistency, consistent names, punctuation, figures/tables and heading terminology; avoid overly polished mechanical repetition.
4. **Local proofread:** fluent native-level or qualified language review where possible, then Word layout audit of all foreign terms, numeric symbols and bidirectional punctuation.
